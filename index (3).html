Analog design

Analog Function Generator

Western University · Dec 2025 · 4-person team

This was a function generator I built from scratch that puts out sine, square, and triangle waves, all from one TL082 op-amp design. It's four stages chained together (an astable multivibrator, an integrator, a sine-shaper, and an inverting amplifier), and I hand-calculated the component values (a 22 nF timing cap and a 6.8 kΩ resistor) to aim for around 3.1 kHz. Before touching any hardware I simulated the whole thing in Micro-Cap to make sure it actually worked.

Once the schematic was final I laid out and fabricated my own PCB, then hand-soldered and populated all the through-hole parts. When I powered it up on a bench supply I checked every stage on a Rigol oscilloscope, measuring the peak-to-peak voltage and frequency at each point. The stages came out between 1.9 and 2.5 kHz instead of the 3.1 kHz I was going for, and I tracked that down to real component tolerances versus the perfect values in simulation, plus a soldering mistake on the first pass that I had to go back and fix. Seeing how far the real board drifted from the simulation was the biggest thing I took away from it.
